# Minimum Changes

**1. Holding.** Hold your move, your Action, some of your attacks, or any mix of
them.

- The rank behind holds its move and steps up when the front foe falls.
- A fighter in a doorway holds his attack and strikes the first one that steps
  into reach.
- An archer holds her shot until her friend is out of the line.
- A Veteran with a longsword uses one attack now and holds the other: "My
  weapon's longer. As he steps up, I stick him."

However, don't expect to always go first. In the longsword example, a spearman foe has a longer reach and will attack first. Compare using item slots.

**2. Fuel.** Your maximum is your level + empty slots (up to 3). Roll your
maximum in round 1. At each Roll Initiative after that, drop any dice you don't
want, then roll new dice until you're back at your maximum. Weapons, shields and
Arcana give no dice. Sorcerie slots don't count as empty.

That's 4 dice at level 1, 5 at level 2, 6 at level 3. With 4 dice you can make
one big two-handed blow and Defend twice, or two big blows and no Defend, or one
attack and Defend three times.

Leave your pack in a room that feels safe. Caught with it on, you roll only your
level in dice. Dropping it mid-fight takes your Action.

**3. Traits matter.** When you burn a 1 using an item you have the Trait for,
reroll it. Any item: a weapon, a shield, an Arcana.

**4. Classic side initiative.** Roll 1d6 per side to see who goes first. (If a
side is split up in the fight, the Referee may give each part its own d6.) A
surprised side goes last in round 1. Light kit is quick: add 1 to your side's
roll for you alone for every 4 empty slots.

**5. Fatigued.** Hard going wears you out. While Fatigued, your Fuel maximum is
halved (round up). An Hour Rest clears it. It doesn't stack.

- A fight that runs 10 rounds (a full Turn) leaves everyone in it Fatigued.
- Six days of travel without a day's rest.
- Anything else that would wear you out, on the Referee's call: you come out of
  the rapids half-drowned, run a long chase, climb all afternoon, go a night
  without sleep.

A Journeyman keeps the group going longer. Add his Craft to the hours or days
before the group he's guiding becomes Fatigued.

Fatigue costs no HP and no Heat. HP is harm, Heat is what you owe, Fatigued is
wear.

**6. Roll monster damage.** The Referee always rolls monster damage, using the
monster's damage die as printed. When an attack die rolls its top face (6 on a
d6, 8 on a d8), roll one more of that die and add it. Only once; the extra die
doesn't roll again. If several dice roll their top face, it's the Referee's
call: when the attack is meant to be deadly, each one adds its own die.

The roll is the most the blow can do. The Referee may always deal less when the
situation calls for it. It's the same blow, not a second attack: one Defend, one touch, one dose
of poison, one Trait Curse.

## Rules text that changes with them

- "You cannot hold an Action or an attack until a trigger" goes. Change 1
  replaces it.
- The round 2 refill (one die per level) is replaced by the top-off.
- "A die is used at its face and discarded. No reroll." gets the Trait as its
  exception.
- Winded, or Fuel you're forced to lose with an empty pool: you top off one die
  short next round.
- Counting 3s and 4s for Initiative, and each foe group rolling its own d6, go.
  Change 4 replaces them.
- Wear stops being paid in Heat. The Referee Guide's Grind section (a long crawl
  raises Heat) and the Journeyman's cold-rain example (6 Heat, cut by Craft)
  become Fatigued, with Craft stretching how long the group lasts.
- Flat monster damage quotes go: the quote table in the Referee Guide's
  *Monsters at speed* and the damage-die-to-quote table in OSE Conversions.
  Change 6 replaces them.

## Watch for in play

- Light kit. Fuel stops at 3 empty slots, so slots past that only buy speed. A
  Burner goes first 42% of the time, 58% with 4 empty slots (+1), 72% with 8
  (+2). It only matters when the party ties or loses by a little.
- Fatigue. Fights should end in 2–4 rounds, so 10 rounds only catches a grind.
  Undead and constructs don't tire. A Fatigued Sorcerer may be short of dice for
  his biggest spells.
