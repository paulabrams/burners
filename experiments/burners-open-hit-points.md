# Open Hit Points

# needs-playtest

What's the name of the man, the hero, who stopped that Kurgan and took the baton? Who is this statue named after?
Monsters show their hit points. No cards, no dividing by 5. The players see the skeleton, see its 10 HP, and knock it down themselves.

## At the table

When you say "Roll Initiative," put the monsters on the slide. Make one card per kind of monster and copy it for each one in the fight:

> **Skeleton** · AC 1 · HP 10 · claws 2 · Cutty −2, arrows −4

**Count damage up, not HP down.** The HP stays printed on the card. Keep a damage track beside each copy and add each blow to it; when the damage reaches the HP, the monster drops. Adding beats subtracting, and every copy stays the same card. On the slide: *Skeleton 2 · 7 / 10*. The players do the math; the track just grows.

**HP = 10 × HD**, or double the module's printed HP if it gives one. Every copy has the same HP, so nobody rolls. The players stack a lot of dice on a blow (the two-hander's second die, Veteran attacks, Sparks, magic), so monsters need the extra.

| Monster | HD | AC | HP |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Skeleton | 1 | 1 | 10 |
| Goblin | 1 | 2 | 10 |
| Wolf | 2 | 1 | 20 |
| Ogre | 4 | 3 | 40 |
| Troll | 6 | 3 | 60 |
| Knight in full plate | 3 | 5 | 30 |

AC comes from the monster's D&D armor class: 9–8 [10–11] is 0, 7 [12] is 1, 6 [13] is 2, 5–4 [14–15] is 3, 3 [16] is 4, 2 [17] or better is 5.

## One blow

**Damage = your dice + your weapon's slots − the monster's AC.** It comes straight off the monster's HP. Zero or less does nothing.

A resistance takes 2 more off what's named, or 4 for what barely touches it. A Bane adds 2.

Work out your number once, when the card goes up: slots minus AC, plus any resistance or Bane. A sword against the troll is −1, a greatsword +1, a dagger against plate −4. After that, every blow is your dice plus that one number.

*A sword (2 slots) hits a skeleton (AC 1) with a 3: 3 + 2 − 1 is 4, and the skeleton's track reads 4 / 10. A greatsword (4) hits a troll (AC 3) with 5 and 4: 9 + 4 − 3 is 10, and the troll's track reads 10 / 60. A dagger (1) hits a knight in full plate (AC 5) with a 4: 4 + 1 − 5 is nothing. Grab the mace.*

Monster armor now works like yours: it soaks every blow. Weapon slots count in full on every blow, with nothing lost to rounding.

## The Trait: no Spark without it

A 6 burned with a weapon or shield you don't have the Trait for counts as a 6, but it can't Spark. No Flurry, Impale, Sever, Man-handle, Past them, or Counterattack with it. Fancy work takes training.

This matches magic: without the Trait, an enchanted weapon's magic stays quiet, and without the Trait, the steel does nothing fancy either.

**Impale against armor.** Impale's Fuel skips the AC: armor doesn't soak it. Fuel Impale with one die, since the other hand is doing the grabbing.

*A Burner with the dagger Trait goes for a knight in full plate (AC 5, 30 HP). He burns a 6: 6 + 1 − 5 is 2. The 6 Sparks. "I step in close, grab him, and stick it between the plates." He Fuels Impale with a 4, which skips the armor: 6 in all, and the knight's track reads 6 / 30. Without the Trait, the same 6 does 2 and nothing more.*

## Pacing against the Monster Stack

Average blows to kill, counting each blow's own dice only (no Sparks, Veteran attacks, or magic). Monster Stack: 2 × HD cards, trained rounding up. Open HP: 10 × HD.

| Monster | Dagger | Sword | Longsword | Greatsword (two dice) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Skeleton (AC 1) | 1.7 → 2.9 | 1.3 → 2.2 | 1.3 → 1.8 | 0.9 → 1.0 |
| Goblin (AC 2) | 3.0 → 4.0 | 1.7 → 2.9 | 1.3 → 2.2 | 0.9 → 1.1 |
| Wolf (AC 1) | 3.4 → 5.7 | 2.7 → 4.4 | 2.7 → 3.6 | 1.8 → 2.0 |
| Ogre (AC 3) | 12 → 24 | 12 → 16 | 6.9 → 11.4 | 3.6 → 5.0 |
| Troll (AC 3) | 18 → 36 | 18 → 24 | 10.3 → 17.1 | 5.5 → 7.5 |
| Knight in full plate (AC 5) | 9 → 60 | 9 → 30 | 9 → 18 | 4.3 → 5.0 |

Blow for blow, one-handed weapons take about half again as long as the stack, and the greatsword lands close to today. The dice the players pile on top close the gap. Small blades against plate are hopeless, which is what the armor is for.

## What stays the same

Fuel, Initiative, Defend, Block, PC armor, Veteran attacks, the two-hander's second die, Sparks for those with the Trait.

## What goes

The Monster Stack, dividing by 5, the ±2 comparison, and the Trait's rounding.

## Open

- Should a weapon's slots add only with the Trait? That would be a second Trait lever, and a wizard's borrowed sword would hit as a bare 1d6.
- Morale when the damage track reaches half the HP, as with half the stack.
- Tactile and chunky is what the cards do best. At a physical table, the damage track could be chips or dice on the monster's card.
- A stronger dagger: once you've Man-handled a foe and hold it, your Stabbity blows against it skip armor until it breaks free. Two Sparks (the grab, then the stab) for a lot of power; start with plain Impale.
