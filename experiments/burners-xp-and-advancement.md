# Burners: Experience & Advancement

**Status: adopted into the rules (Oct 7, 2026)** — [[Burners Experience]], *Gaining Traits* in [[Burners Adventure Game]], [[Burners Burn Undead]], plus the FAQ, OSE Conversions, Principles, and Referee Guide. This file stays as the design record and the Lothian worked example.

**Revised in the rules (Oct 8, 2026):** Trait slots and pending Traits are gone. Traits are gained on waking from sleep, one or several, up to the four for your next level, from what happened at the table. The Referee tells a player when they level. Trait Drain now curses a Trait (newest first; its 1s hate you) instead of removing it. The Trait sections below are kept as history.

Design notes for a revised XP rule. Burners already pays treasure XP on claim rather than on the trip home, and already gates levels on XP plus four Traits. This note changes which treasure pays, when it pays, and who keeps track of what.

## What Burners Does Now

From [[Burners Experience]] as written:

- 1 XP per gp when treasure is claimed: taken and named, counted, or stowed. Hauling it home is not the trigger.
- Monster XP when a foe is overcome: killed, routed, or otherwise put down.
- Magic items, keeps, ships, and monster parts give no XP.
- A level needs both the XP and the four Traits for that level, and you gain at most one Trait per session.
- The XP table is cumulative, so nothing is lost by passing a line before you have the Traits.
- The Referee keeps the totals and tells a player when asked.

## The Problem

- **Players prefer fighting to looting**, which is on-theme for Burners, but claim-based XP pays the treasure far more than the fights that matter.
- **Finder vs. earner resentment.** Claiming credits whoever is standing there when the loot is taken, not whoever risked their lives for it. In Lothian, the 40 gp under the Drowned Lord's coffin went to six characters in Session 6, two of whom joined long after he died. The platinum torc beside it was seen in Session 1, left behind, and washed away, so the three who killed the Lord got nothing for it.
- **Players constantly ask "do we have enough XP?"**, which turns advancement into scorekeeping.

## The Test: Wrested from the Dungeon

XP comes from treasure wrested from the grasp of the dungeon (a monster, a trap, a lock, the tide, the deep itself) and from the foes overcome there.

Town and civilization pay coin, never XP. Robbing people in town, looting a camp you scared off, murdering townsfolk, or killing their farm animals earns nothing on the ledger. The wreckers' crates in Lothian are the case in point: taken from people, so gold if anything, and no XP.

Foes who come at you with steel out in the wild, such as bandits, raiders, and pirates, are foes like any monster, and what they guard counts.

## The Deed Pays, Not the Finding

XP is awarded for the **deed** that wrested the treasure free, never for possession, recovery, or discovery.

- Defeat a monster **where it keeps its hoard** and you earn its XP plus the XP for everything in its grasp, at that moment, whether or not anyone ever finds it. You must be **in the lair** or in extremely close proximity to the treasure: the same chamber, the coffin it rose from, the nest at its back. Being in the same dungeon is not enough.
- Defeat a monster **away from its hoard** (at the lair's mouth, in the next corridor, or a hundred miles off) and you earn its XP only. The hoard stays in the dungeon, uncredited, until someone wrests it free.
- **A gatekeeper guards the door, not the dungeon.** Kill the guardian at the entrance and you earn its XP and whatever it kept at the gate. The rooms beyond pay only when you go in and wrest their treasure free.
- Sneak past a guardian and you earn XP only for what you carry off, when you take it. Nothing for the guardian, nothing for what you leave.
- **A trap guards what lies behind it.** Get past a trap, a lock, or a deadly crossing that guards a room, and stand in that room, and you earn XP for the treasure in it, whether you take it or leave it. Beating the trap is the deed, the same as killing a guardian in its lair. The same proximity applies: the room the trap guards, not the whole dungeon beyond it. **The trap itself pays nothing.** Traps have no XP of their own, so a deadly trap guarding an empty corridor earns no XP. Beating it only matters for what lies behind it, which keeps players hunting treasure rather than hunting traps to spring.
- Find another way around a trap, without facing it, and it's the same as sneaking past a guardian: XP only for what you carry off.
- Treasure the dungeon itself holds with nothing guarding its room (loose in the silt of a flooding barrow, under the tide) pays when taken, to those there.
- **Undefended treasure pays nothing.** If nothing at all stands between the world and a hoard, carrying it off is just picking it up. A stranger who wanders into a dead dragon's empty lair gets the gold and no XP.

### Staking a Claim

One exception keeps a long adventure from ending in nothing. **Enter a lair while its guardian lives, and come out alive, and you have staked a claim on that hoard.** If the guardian later dies or leaves by any means, those who staked the claim earn the hoard's XP when they go back and take it. Nobody else ever does.

The test is a plain fact from play: were you in the lair while the guardian was alive? How the guardian died doesn't matter. Nobody has to argue that their plan lured it out on purpose, or that someone else's kill was really their doing. If you braved the lair, the hoard is yours to earn. If you didn't, it isn't.

- The treasure itself is its own reward. Finding it, recovering it, and carrying it off gives you the gold to spend, but no additional XP.

### Credited Once

**Each gold piece is credited to the party exactly once**, at the deed that earned it, to the characters present for that deed.

If someone later finds loot that was already credited, there is no new XP. At the table, the answer is simply: *"That was already credited to the people who wrested it free."*

| Situation | Kill XP | Treasure XP |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Defeat the monster beside its hoard and find the hoard | Yes | Yes, at the kill |
| Defeat the monster beside its hoard, never find the hoard | Yes | Yes, at the kill |
| Defeat the monster far from its hoard | Yes | No; the hoard waits in the dungeon |
| Defeat the guardian at the entrance, never go in | Yes | Only what it kept at the gate |
| Sneak past the monster and take part of the hoard | No | Only what you took, when you take it |
| Get past the trap or lock guarding a room and enter it | — | Yes, all treasure in that room, taken or not |
| Beat a trap with nothing behind it | — | No; traps pay nothing themselves |
| Go around the trap without facing it | — | Only what you carry off |
| Take treasure loose in the dungeon, its room unguarded | — | Yes, when taken |
| Guardian died elsewhere; you had braved its lair while it lived | — | Yes, when you take the hoard |
| Guardian died elsewhere; you never faced it in its lair | — | No; the gold is yours, the XP isn't |
| Later stumble on already-credited loot | No | No |
| Rob or kill people in town | No | No |

### The Hobbit, by These Rules

- **The trolls.** Gandalf's trick with the dawn turns them to stone. That is overcome, same as a sword. They fall beside their cave, so the cave's gold is credited at the dawn. The company would have earned that XP even if they never found the cave; finding it gave them the gold.
- **Glamdring, Orcrist, and Sting.** Magic items. Their own reward, no XP.
- **Bilbo and the cup.** He sneaks past sleeping Smaug and carries off one cup. XP for the cup, when he takes it. Nothing for Smaug and nothing for the rest of the hoard.
- **Bard and Smaug.** Bard kills the dragon over Lake-town, nowhere near the Mountain. Monster XP for Bard and those with him, and no hoard XP: he never entered the lair. The dwarves staked their claim when they went into the Mountain with Smaug alive and Bilbo carried the cup out from under him. When they walk back in and take the hoard, they earn its XP. Whether they meant to send the dragon at the town never comes up.
- **A stranger at the Mountain.** Had some passing adventurer found the hoard undefended after Smaug died, they would get the gold and no XP. They never faced the dragon in his lair.
- **The chimera.** It flies a hundred miles from its lair to burn a town, and a hero kills it there without knowing the lair exists. Monster XP only. Whoever later finds the empty lair gets the gold and no XP, unless they had braved it while the chimera lived.

### Attendance

XP is **attendance-gated**. A player absent from a session has a character who wasn't there, and that character earns nothing from that session's deeds. Splits follow [[Burners Experience]] as written: Burners and non-Mustering retainers in the divisor, retainers keep half, and Mustering seats split half of their Boss's share.

### GM Ledger

The GM's master treasure list doubles as the XP ledger. When keying, name what each foe guards: the treasure within its reach, not the whole dungeon behind it. Each hoard belongs to the nearest guardian; when in doubt, it is the hoard in the creature's own lair. Record the **full value** of everything a monster guarded at the moment of the kill, so a later partial discovery never prompts a recount. Already-credited treasure that is still lying in the dungeon gets a line saying who it was credited to, so finding it later is a gold-only event.

## Making Worthy Kills Pay

Some of the most important foes (undead, demons, plague-beasts) have no fictional reason to sit on a pile of gold. Two ways they pay:

- **The kill itself.** Set the monster's XP to match how dangerous it was. Don't shortchange a foe because it carries nothing. Lothian's draugr was first logged at 35 XP, far too low for an HD 5 undead, and now pays 335.
- **Actual hoards**: where the fiction supports a creature having treasure.

Bounties and monster parts are not among them. Both pay gold only; see below.

## Their Own Reward

Some things you win in the dungeon are useful or sellable in themselves. They give **no XP**, whether you take them or leave them. The XP for them was already paid by the deed (the kill, the trap, the lock), or there was never any to pay.

- **Magic items.** They pay out in capability rather than points. This avoids having to price a flaming sword in gold equivalents, and it fits the existing principle that tuned weapons are earned in play. Enchanted fixtures count as magic. The silver bands in the Lothian gate-thing cell carry Umbrakala binding runes, so they are a working enchantment, not bullion: 0 XP. Pried loose and sold, they are worth coin, not XP.
- **Weapons, armor, and tools.** Recovered blades, harness, shields, helms, rope, lanterns, crowbars, boats: you got the thing, and the thing is the reward. Sell a heap of captured cutlasses and the coin is purse money, not XP. Lothian's crab-cave weapons and metal hats, the bone-fighters' bronze, and the pirate cutlasses in the Wet Devil's nest all paid 0 XP.
- **Monster parts.** Hide, tusks, venom, a draugr's salt-grey dust. The kill's XP already includes whatever the body was worth, so cutting it up doesn't pay again. That also keeps parts out of the deed rule: if parts paid XP, you'd earn it at the kill whether or not anyone harvested them, so it may as well just be part of the kill. Some parts sell for gold; the Referee sets the price when someone asks, and it's purse money. Lothian's vampire dust was first paid as 300 coin XP and reclassified onto the draugr's kill, which now pays 335.
- **Bounties.** A town, temple, or patron pays a price for a deed, such as a monster's head. That's town coin, and town pays coin, never XP. The kill already paid its XP; the bounty is purse money on top.
- **Ships, keeps, and land.** You hold them; that's the reward.

Players track their own loot. If nobody writes down that they harvested the gall bladder, they didn't take it.

## Leveling: Two Gates

To advance a level, a character needs **both**:

1. **All four Traits** for the next level, and
2. **Enough XP**.

Characters begin at level 1 with four Traits. To become eligible for level 2, they must earn four more Traits through play. This is already Burners as written.

### Hidden XP

**The XP total is never revealed to players.** The Referee tracks XP and nothing else. Players track their own Traits, their pending Trait slots and the moments that will fill them, the sessions they attended, and their level. When a character has all four Traits, the player asks *"Do I have enough?"* and the GM answers yes or not yet.

This only removes the XP anxiety if "not yet" doesn't drag on. At Lothian's pace XP is the gate that binds, not Traits (see the worked example), so a character can hold four Traits for many sessions. If "not yet" repeats, answer with a rough sense of distance ("about halfway", "close") rather than the number.

### Carry-Over

XP is cumulative in Burners, so leftover XP carries past a level-up and nothing is lost by not watching the total. That is what makes it safe to keep the number behind the screen.

## Earning Traits

### Trait Slots

Each session you attend earns one **Trait slot**.

- A slot you haven't filled yet is a **pending Trait**. You fill it when you have a table moment to point to (see the Table-Event Rule below).
- Pending Traits **accumulate up to a cap of four**, which is one level's worth.
- This forgives short sessions and nights where the fiction didn't offer a natural opening, without letting anyone stockpile an unlimited backlog.
- A player who doesn't develop their character doesn't advance; that's on them.
- The player keeps the count, not the Referee.
- A Trait aimed at a new Approach commits your next level there. Change your mind and you discard that Trait and earn a different one, so you can feel out a path before you commit to it.

For example, a level 1 character starts with four Traits. Over her next four sessions she earns four slots and fills two of them. She has **six Traits and two pending**. She needs both pending slots filled, and the XP, to reach level 2.

### Trait Drain and Pending Traits

Some undead drain Traits instead of levels (see [[Burners Burn Undead#Level Drain > Trait Drain]]). **Trait Drain takes a Trait you've selected, never a pending slot.** The dead feed on what you've become, not on the sessions you showed up for.

- In the example above, a wight's touch takes one of her six Traits; any of them can go, starting Traits included (the Referee chooses, rolls, or lets the player pick). She has five Traits and still two pending.
- A drained Trait can be earned back like any other Trait, by filling a slot with it.
- **Signatures sit outside the drain.** If a drained Trait had a Signature, the Signature stays in effect. Drain is frightening because it can kill, not because it can take a named sword or a companion.
- **Drained of every selected Trait, the character is slain** and rises as the kind of undead that drained them. Pending slots don't count; there's nothing in them to hold on to.
- Leaving slots pending buys no protection. The drain still takes from what you have, and an empty slot fills no faster after a drain than before.

### The Table-Event Rule

**Every Trait must be grounded in an event that actually happened at the table.** No pure between-session narration ("I've been practicing on the road").

However, **the Trait doesn't have to be declared in that moment.** Whenever a player claims a Trait, they reference the witnessed moment that earned it:

> *"Remember three sessions ago when I talked the guard down at the gate? That's where I earned Silver Tongue."*

- **One moment justifies one Trait.** A single dramatic scene can't be mined for multiple Traits; each claim spends its moment.
- Players jot the moment down when it happens, so "three sessions ago" isn't a memory contest.

## Player-Facing Summary

Say this out loud at the table, clearly, because it's the rule that changes player behavior and it only works if they trust it:

> Earn your four Traits through play, and when you claim one, point to the moment at the table that earned it. XP comes from treasure you wrest out of the dungeon and from the foes you beat there, not from robbing folk in town. If you beat a monster right where it keeps its treasure, you get XP for that treasure whether or not you ever find it; finding the treasure means you get to keep it. Sneak past it and you get XP for what you carry off. Get past a trap into the room it guards and the same goes: you've earned what's in that room. If you braved a monster's lair while it lived and something else kills it later, its hoard is still yours to earn; a hoard you find lying undefended is gold, not XP. Magic items, weapons, armor, tools, monster parts, and bounties are their own reward: useful or worth coin, never XP. When you have all four Traits, ask me if you have enough XP to level.

## Worked Example: The Lothian Campaign (through Session 8)

What the Lothian totals would be under this rule. Every award already on the [XP log](../../../campaigns/Lothian%20Campaign/campaign-player-briefs/lothian-xp-log.md) stands; under credited-once, nothing is clawed back. New credits are guarded treasure the party never took.

**Platinum torc, 100 gp.** In the Drowned Lord's coffin. Reinhardt, Herrick, and Bellemy killed him beside it in Session 0. ÷3, **33** each. The torc has since washed down into the barrow; whoever finds it gets the gold only.

**Grey Hart cargo, 6,250 gp.** Credited at the Wet Devil kill in Session 3: she was the jailer who sorted the ship's loot in her bubble, killed in her own lair. Nobody has ever searched the hold. Gott-silver ~4,000, gilded spyglass 250, the master's gold seal-ring 250, strongbox 350, spice 400, pine timber 1,000. ÷7 (five Burners plus Ylva and Gar as non-Mustering retainers), **892** each, retainers half, **446**. Spade counts though he held the donkey on the beach; he was on the job. Torvi was hired later.

The Referee raised this cargo after Session 8, about thirteen times its first keying. The ship was never searched, so nothing the players saw changed. The keyed treasure was too thin for the pace he wants, and the deed rule makes raising unfound treasure an honest lever: the players earn the XP for the deed they already did, and finding the gold is still ahead of them. Under the claim rule, the same boost would sit unpaid in a hold nobody has a reason to search.

**Weeping Beauty tea services, 1,500 gp.** Three exquisite services, porcelain and gold leaf, one on each of three tables, **500** each. In the room behind the Maiden's gate trap, which Herrick, Egil, Aren, and Kai'Shem beat roped together in Session 8, with Egil's Muster. ÷4, **375** each. The Mustered seats split half of Egil's share, **62** each. Left on the tables; credited anyway.

Not credited:

- The heart of the Kalla in the canopic jar at the center of Weeping Beauty: magic, 0 XP. So are the seeds and the pomegranate.
- The gate-thing cell's silver bands are enchanted: magic, 0 XP.
- The wreckers' crates were taken from people: no XP.
- The ship itself, the bronze on the bone-fighters, and the skiff's oilcloth map: kit, ships, and lore with no gp value.
- The 40 gp coffin sack (Session 6) and the 12 gp crab-cave gold (Session 3) went to their finders under the old rule. Credited once; they stay where they are.

Session 8 (Sep 30) is the first time the trap rule applies. The Maiden's gate is trapped: the step out of the bubbles drops onto a too-narrow stair over a fifty-foot fall, 5d6 damage and certain death for any first-level character. The party roped together and came through, so the three tea services in Weeping Beauty are credited to those who entered, whether or not they carry them out. The silver bands in the crypts are magic (0 XP).

The next fight is where the kill rule first bites. The Dying Strength gladiators wear gold-filigree skullcaps; under the deed rule those caps are credited at the kill, to whoever is on the stair, whether or not anyone stops to pick them up.

At the table, Saxon put the problem plainly: "by the numbers so far, stealing is way more profitable from an XP perspective," and "we don't seem to get treasure when I'm here." Aren and Kai'Shem joined after the barrow's big hauls were claimed, so the claim rule has paid them almost nothing. Under the deed rule the tea services alone take each of them from double digits past 400, for beating the gate in their first or second dive, and the next hoard no longer goes to whoever happens to be standing there when it's picked up.

Pace: under the old rule the leaders are at about 735 of 2,000 after nine sessions, roughly 80 XP a session, and level 2 is a dozen or more sessions away, long after four Traits are in hand. The deed rule alone, on the treasure as first keyed, only took Herrick to 1,200. With the Grey Hart cargo raised, Herrick crosses 2,000 and Egil is at about 1,900, with the gladiators' skullcaps still to come. The four original characters land on level 2 around Session 9 or 10, close to the one-level-every-four-sessions cadence the Referee wants. Holding that pace means about 500 XP per character per session, which with five Burners sharing is roughly 2,500 gp of creditable treasure a session at level 1, doubling with each level.

## Open Questions for Playtest

- Does the XP pace actually ripen alongside the four-session Trait gate, or do players routinely hit four Traits while still short on XP? Lothian says XP is the slower gate by a wide margin; treasure density or the level 2 line may need tuning.
- Do players find enough table moments to support their Traits, or do players often sit at the cap of four pending slots?
- Does monster XP pay enough for treasure-less foes, or does the conversion need tuning upward? The draugr at 35 suggests the shortcut chart runs low for undead with special powers.
- Does "in the lair or extremely close" need a firmer edge? The Drowned Lord beside his coffin counts; a gatekeeper at the entrance does not own the rooms behind it; a chimera a hundred miles off does not own its lair. Cases in between are the Referee's call. A hoard whose guardian died elsewhere pays only those who staked a claim on it.
