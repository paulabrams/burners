# Danger Check

Counting Turns is a chore, but without time pressure the crew takes its sweet time. This drops the clock. The place has a life of its own, and the Referee checks on it out loud whenever the crew gives it a reason: dawdling, noise, bright light. Something is always closing in. The red die is the monsters; the black die is the dark, eating the crew's light and spells (the torch stands for everything that runs down). Nothing is written down, and nobody converts checks back into minutes.

## At the table

When the crew dawdles, makes noise, or enters a new area, roll red and black.

- **Red 1:** roll red again. At or under the dungeon level: wandering monster.
- **Black 1:** roll black again. 2+, torches out. 5+, lantern dry. Over a spell's level, it ends.
- **Both 1:** wandering monster, and the lights die.

Red counts on 1–2 if they're reckless; black counts on 1–2 if it's wet, windy, a long wait, or the dark is hungry.

## The rule

**Check when they give the place a reason.** The Referee throws the red and black dice together where everyone hears it when the crew:

- **dawdles:** searching, picking a lock, resting, a long argument at the door;
- **makes noise:** forcing a door, a fight, shouting, a spell going off;
- **makes a show of light,** or the light fails;
- **moves on** into a new area, or a fight ends with someone Wounded.

A check might stand for five minutes or thirty. That's the point: it measures how much the crew has stirred the place, not the time on a clock.

**Each die counts on a 1, or wider when the crew pushes its luck. When it counts, roll it again to back it up.**

| Die | Counts on | Backup roll |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Monster die** (red) | 1 normally; 1–2 or 1–3 when the crew is reckless | At or under the **dungeon level:** a **wandering monster,** or a trap or other hazard. Set how close from the fiction, as now. |
| **Torch die** (black) | 1 normally; 1–2 or 1–3 when the place is hard on light | **Consumables.** Anything whose **Durability** the backup beats ends: lit torches gutter out, a lantern flask runs dry, a spell fades. |

Either, both, or neither can come up on the same check.

**Double 1s.** If red and black both show 1 on the first throw (1 in 36), skip both backups: a wandering monster comes, and every light and spell in use fails as it arrives. It's the Double Spicy of the check.

**The monster die** counts on a 1. Widen it to 1–2 when the crew is reckless (very loud, a fight, a spell going off, kicking in a door), 1–3 when they're very reckless. Then the backup: at or under the dungeon level, something comes. Level 1 hits on a 1, level 3 on 1–3, level 6 and below on 1–6. Add one or two to the level when the dungeon is alerted and hunting the crew (never past 6). Say so when it changes; the players should feel the place turn.

**The torch die** counts on a 1 for an ordinary check: dry, still, quick. Widen it to 1–2 when the place is hard on light, and to 1–3 when two of these stack (running through the rain):

- **Wet:** rain, a dripping cave, mist, wading water.
- **Wind:** drafts down a shaft, open sky, a gale on a cliff path.
- **Bad air:** deep mines, smoke-filled rooms, foul or thin air where flames burn low.
- **Rough handling:** running, climbing, swimming, or fighting with the torch in hand.
- **A long wait:** a long search, an Hour Rest, lying in ambush.
- **Hungry dark:** places that eat light, like an undead tomb where the darkness claws at your torches, a shadow-haunted hall, or anywhere Umbrakala has soaked in. These wear down spells as well as flames.

**Durability.** Each consumable has a Durability from 0 to 5. When the torch die counts, roll the backup once for everything in use: anything with a Durability below the roll ends. A 2 puts out the torches but leaves the lantern; a 6 ends everything.

| Consumable | Durability |
| --- | --- |
| Rag-on-a-stick | 0: goes out whenever the torch die counts |
| Torch, candle | 1 |
| Lantern flask | 4 |
| Spell that lasts Turns | its spell level |

| Durability | Ends on | Chance a check (torch range 1) | Lasts about |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | any | 1 in 6 | 6 checks |
| 1 | 2–6 | 5 in 36 | 7 checks |
| 2 | 3–6 | 1 in 9 | 9 checks |
| 3 | 4–6 | 1 in 12 | 12 checks |
| 4 | 5–6 | 1 in 18 | 18 checks |
| 5 | 6 | 1 in 36 | 36 checks |

| Dungeon level | Chance a check (monster die on a 1) |
| --- | --- |
| 1 (level 1) | 1 in 36 |
| 1–3 (level 3) | 1 in 12 |
| 1–6 (level 6 and below) | 1 in 6 |

## Why it might work

- **No clock.** The Referee checks when the crew does something that matters to the place, so a quick, quiet crew hears the dice rarely and a dawdling, noisy one hears them over and over.
- **Each number has one job.** The first rolls carry what the crew did and where they are (recklessness on red; weather, wet, and waiting on black). The backups carry what they're up against: the dungeon level on red, the gear's Durability on black.
- **Both dice work the same way.** Count on a 1, widen it when the crew pushes its luck, roll again to back it up.
- **Two dice, no competition.** Both are read every check, so a hunted crew doesn't burn torches faster for it, a windy shaft eats light without bringing monsters, and neither result crowds out the other.
- **One backup, staggered endings.** A single torch backup sorts everything in use: torches go first, the lantern holds longer, a strong spell outlasts both.
- **Deeper is busier.** The level sets the backup with nothing to look up.
- **Dawdling has a sound.** A long debate is a check, and the dice hit the table.

## Watch for

- **The shallow levels are quiet.** On level 1 a monster is 1 in 36 a check. A reckless crew widens the first roll and checks more often, so it still finds trouble. If level 1 feels too safe, start the count at 1–2.
- **Torches go out together.** Every lit torch shares a Durability, so they gutter at once. That's a scramble in the dark, which is the point, but harsher than staggered torches. If it feels wrong, only the oldest torch goes out.
- **Rag-on-a-stick.** It's as short-lived as anything on this check gets; let the fiction put it out sooner.
- **Spells last longer.** A level 1 spell lasts about seven checks, where Burners spells now mostly last "Sorcerie turns" (1 to 3 Turns). Spells that say "the scene" or "until washed" keep their own ending. If the caster should matter, Durability could be the caster's Sorcerie instead of the spell level, which fits the "Sorcerie turns" wording.
- **What earns a check.** A quick look or opening a door doesn't. A search, a lock, a rest, a debate that goes round twice, a loud act, or a new area does.
- **Players not noticing.** Wåndyr's bowl clock failed because players didn't connect the dice to time (below). Say what the check was for: "You argued at the door. Torches gutter."

## From *Into the Odd*

*Into the Odd* (Remastered, 2022): "the Referee rolls d6 each time the group moves into a new area, loiters, or makes loud noise. A roll of 1 means the group encounters something, a roll of 2 means they hear something approaching or nearby, and 3–6 means there is no encounter." Turns are abstract, light has no set lifespan, and a short rest restores HP but counts as loitering, so stopping is the risk. *Electric Bastionland* keeps it: roll when the crew explores, rests, or hesitates. This version keeps the new-area, loitering, and noise cues as reasons to check and adds a second die for the light.

## From Wåndyr

Wåndyr tried most of the options first:

- **Tension Pool** (dev archive): add a die each wasted Turn, roll the pool on reckless acts, clear at six. Didn't resonate at the table.
- **Turn Clock** (`encounter-oracle/Wåndyr Turn Clock.md`): a metal bowl, a die tossed in per Turn, roll and clear at six. The playtest notes found players didn't notice it; clearing the bowl was quieter than filling it. Spells lasted until the next die went in.
- **The playtest's answer** (`Turn Clock Playtesting.md`): "Ditch turn clock entirely… Roll 1d6 each turn, wandering monster on 1… Sometimes simple is best." A later idea there rolled 2d6 and used doubles for a trap.
- **Encounter Oracle** (after *The Black Hack*): a d6 table of danger, foreshadowing, signs of near and far life, environmental change, and time passing (light used, fatigue, a brief rest), rolled every 15 minutes of real time or when the crew lingers or makes noise. Roll a d6 first, then a second die on the matching table, a two-step shape like the backup rolls here. Its distance table runs 1 ambush through 6 near miss.
- **Camp time** (playtest notes): the other half of the pace problem. Long side actions (studying an item, experimenting with spells) wait for camp: "That sounds time-consuming, wait for camp?" Adventure time stays quick; camp time has no clock.

## What it would replace

In the [[Burners Referee Guide]]: the Turn Tracker count and its even-turn check, light tracked on paper ("do not hide it behind a die"), and the optional Delve Die, whose any-1 encounter runs about 1 in 3. The separate noise, dark, and wounds checks become reasons for the same check. Light durations in hours give way to Durability. Torches would also drop out of [[burners-usage-die]]; rations and arrows stay there.
