# Placed Spells as Friction

A spell that doesn't name a creature is placed in the world: a web across a doorway, ice on the stairs, a fog. It catches no one on its own. Anyone can walk into it, go around it, or wait it out.

## The proposal

Aiming a placed spell at a creature is Friction. Burn one Fuel to try, and you can't burn again. If the creature counters with more Fuel, the spell goes awry: the creature slips free, or the spell lands in the wrong spot, maybe on your own people. Each creature you aim at is its own try.

- "Names a creature" means *a creature*, *the target*, *one animal*, or a number of them. Command, Healing Touch, and the like are not placed spells and don't need this.
- The Referee may give a creature a free die to counter when the spell is a poor match: an ogre against a web, a bronze man against water.
- A caught creature Defends against the spell as normal.

## Why Friction

It's a rule players already know. It's one die, and the target gets a say. It also fixes the Lothian web without a new price: four skeletons is four Fuel, each one a chance for a skeleton to tear loose.

## Through your own line

Casting a web past your front line onto the skeletons behind them shouldn't cost twice: a 6 for In the line of fire, then Friction for each skeleton.

Lean: a placed spell appears where you can see. It doesn't travel, so In the line of fire doesn't apply. That rule is for things that fly: arrows, bolts, a thrown flask, a spell that shoots. You pay only the Friction for each creature, and the line still matters: if a skeleton counters, the web may land on your own fighters.

## Open

- Does the counter replace the Defend, or come before it? Lean: before it. Friction decides whether you caught it; the Defend decides how much the spell does.
- Should monsters counter from their pool for this? Lean: yes, like any Friction.
